# Spatial transcriptomics dataset — origin, citation, QC and preprocessing

## Methods paragraph

Spatial transcriptomic data were obtained from the publicly available dataset GSE288365 (Gene
Expression Omnibus), generated by Zhang et al. and reported in Zhang, Z. *et al.* Integrated
single-cell and spatial transcriptomic profiling in ALS uncovers peripheral-to-central immune
infiltration and reprogramming. *Nature Neuroscience* **29**, 1735–1748 (2026);
doi:10.1038/s41593-026-02300-5. The dataset comprises 10x Genomics Visium spatial gene expression
with simultaneous 35-plex antibody-derived tag (protein) capture, applied to post-mortem human
spinal cord. Tissue sections were imaged by immunofluorescence for IBA1, MAP2 and TDP-43 rather
than by haematoxylin and eosin. We analysed all 20 sections from 18 donors — 9 non-neurological
controls, 5 patients with sporadic ALS (including one with concomitant Alzheimer's disease and
one with frontotemporal dementia) and 4 patients carrying the *C9orf72* hexanucleotide repeat
expansion; two donors contributed two sections each. Because the assay is probe-based, the panel
comprises 18,085 targeted gene features and by design contains no ribosomal protein genes.

Raw count matrices and spatial coordinates were downloaded from GEO and processed with Scanpy.
Spots were first restricted to those under tissue (`in_tissue == 1`), then filtered on the RNA
modality using thresholds adapted to the low depth typical of post-mortem cord Visium: at least
200 UMIs and 100 detected genes per spot, and no more than 30% mitochondrial reads. Genes detected
in fewer than three spots were removed, leaving 18,067 genes. Of 148,435 in-tissue spots,
132,781 (89.5%) passed quality control, with a median of 724 UMIs, 542 genes and 3.1%
mitochondrial content per spot. Counts were library-size normalised and log-transformed
(log1p counts per 10,000) for spot-level analyses.

To avoid relying on our own tissue segmentation, we integrated the authors' published per-spot
metadata by barcode: manual histological region annotations (anterior horns, other grey matter,
anterolateral white matter, posterior white matter/dorsal columns, meninges and border),
TDP-43 pathology neighbourhood labels, and cell-type abundance estimates. The latter were
produced by the original authors with Cell2location (v0.1.3) against a human spinal cord
single-nucleus reference, using 7 cells per location, a detection alpha of 20 and 5,000 training
epochs, summarised at the 5% posterior quantile and expressed as percentages across 13 cell
types. An earlier marker-score-based regional annotation of our own was tested against these
manual labels, found to disagree substantially, and discarded; all regional analyses reported
here use the authors' manual annotation. For gene-level and module-level comparisons across
conditions, counts were aggregated into pseudobulk profiles by donor and anatomical region
(72 samples × 18,067 genes), converted to log counts per million, and — where co-expression
structure was assessed — centred within region to remove the dominant anatomical axis of
variation.
