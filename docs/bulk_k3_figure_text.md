# Text for Figure: bulk co-expression clustering and functional annotation of the 118 C9-driven survival mediators

---

## 1. Figure caption

**Figure X. The 118 C9-driven survival mediators resolve into three cell-type-associated co-expression programs in bulk cervical spinal cord.**
(**a**) Gene–gene Pearson correlation matrix for all 118 mediator genes across 156 cervical cord RNA-seq samples, ordered by Ward hierarchical clustering on 1 − *r*. Coloured boxes mark the three clusters obtained by cutting the dendrogram at *k* = 3: a myeloid/DAM cluster (66 genes, red), an astrocyte cluster (30 genes, green) and an oligodendrocyte/myelin cluster (22 genes, blue). The myeloid block is strongly internally correlated and anti-correlated with both glial blocks, which correlate positively with each other. (**b–d**) Over-representation analysis of each cluster against curated gene-set libraries, showing representative terms spanning cell-identity (CellMarker, PanglaoDB, HDSigDB) and function (Gene Ontology, KEGG, Reactome, MSigDB Hallmark). Bars give −log₁₀ FDR (Benjamini–Hochberg, genome background); the dotted line marks FDR = 0.05. In-bar labels give the source library and the number of cluster genes in the term. Clustering used bulk expression only; no spatial or single-cell data contributed to cluster definition.

## 2. Methods

**Co-expression clustering.** The 118 genes previously nominated as mediators of the *C9orf72* repeat expansion effect on survival were clustered using their variance-stabilised, batch-corrected expression across 156 human cervical spinal cord RNA-seq samples. A gene × gene Pearson correlation matrix was computed across samples, converted to a distance matrix as 1 − *r*, and clustered by Ward linkage (`scipy.cluster.hierarchy`).

**Choice of cluster number.** The number of clusters was selected using two internal criteria applied to the bulk data alone. First, each split in the dendrogram was tested against 2,000 random bipartitions of the same parent cluster, using the difference between mean within-child and mean between-child correlation as the statistic. Second, for each *k* we computed the proportion of variance explained by the first principal component (the module eigengene) within every cluster, and recorded the value for the least coherent cluster. Mean silhouette width was computed on the same distance matrix for reference. Clustering stability was assessed by leave-one-sample-out refitting (156 refits) and summarised as the fraction of gene pairs whose co-clustering status was preserved.

**Functional annotation.** Over-representation analysis was performed with Enrichr (`gseapy` v1.3.0) across 14 libraries covering Gene Ontology (Biological Process, Molecular Function, Cellular Component), KEGG, Reactome, WikiPathways, MSigDB Hallmark, and cell-type/tissue references (CellMarker 2024, PanglaoDB, Azimuth, Descartes, HDSigDB, Jensen TISSUES, Human Gene Atlas). Each cluster was tested twice: against the genome background, and against the 118 mediator genes as background, the latter asking what distinguishes each cluster from the remaining mediators rather than from the transcriptome. Terms with fewer than three cluster genes were discarded. *P* values were corrected within library by the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure.

**Cross-platform comparison.** The clustering was repeated independently on 72 donor × anatomical-region pseudobulk samples from Visium spatial transcriptomics of human spinal cord, restricted to the 104 mediator genes detectable on that platform and using identical distance and linkage settings. Agreement was quantified by the adjusted Rand index and by per-gene label concordance.

## 3. Results and interpretation

**Three co-expression programs.** Ward clustering of the 118 mediator genes across 156 cervical cord samples resolved a dominant split between a 66-gene myeloid block and a 52-gene glial block, with the glial block subdividing into 30 astrocyte-associated and 22 oligodendrocyte-associated genes (Fig. Xa). Every split tested highly significantly against random bipartitions of its parent cluster (z = 61.5, 17.7 and 10.2 for the first three splits; all *p* ≤ 0.0015), but effect sizes fell sharply and then plateaued below z = 6.5, indicating that the interpretable structure is exhausted within the first few divisions. The proportion of variance captured by the least coherent cluster's eigengene rose from 0.36 (one cluster) to 0.41 (two) and 0.53 (three), and then plateaued at 0.54 through *k* = 6; three clusters is therefore the smallest solution in which every cluster is adequately summarised by a single eigengene, which is the representation required for downstream mediation modelling. Clustering was highly stable under leave-one-sample-out resampling (mean co-clustering agreement 0.994). Mean silhouette width is maximised at two clusters (0.507 versus 0.454 at three), reflecting that the myeloid–glial contrast is the single dominant axis of variation; silhouette cannot register that the glial block comprises two distinct cell-type programs, which the eigengene criterion does.

**Cell identity.** All three clusters were confirmed against curated brain cell-type signatures (Fig. Xb–d): the myeloid cluster against CellMarker microglial cell (27/66 genes, FDR = 2 × 10⁻²⁴), the astrocyte cluster against HDSigDB astrocyte-specific genes (17/30, FDR = 3 × 10⁻¹²) and the oligodendrocyte cluster against HDSigDB oligodendrocyte-specific genes (16/22, FDR = 4 × 10⁻¹⁴). These assignments survive the stringent test of using the 118 mediators themselves as background (FDR = 4 × 10⁻⁹, 2 × 10⁻⁸ and 3 × 10⁻⁹ respectively), establishing that each cluster is enriched for its cell type relative to the other mediators and not merely relative to the transcriptome. Independent clustering of Visium spatial pseudobulk from human spinal cord, restricted to the 104 mediator genes detectable on that platform, recovered the same three clusters, with 94 of 104 genes (90.4%) receiving an identical label and an adjusted Rand index of 0.728.

**Function beyond cell identity.** The myeloid cluster is specifically a granule and lysosomal-secretion program rather than a generic activation signature: its strongest functional term is Reactome neutrophil degranulation (22/66 genes, FDR = 3 × 10⁻¹⁷), supported by GO secretory granule membrane (15 genes, FDR = 8 × 10⁻¹³) and tertiary granule (13 genes, FDR = 6 × 10⁻¹³), with complement signalling also enriched (Hallmark, 7 genes, FDR = 1 × 10⁻⁴). Despite the name, this is not evidence of neutrophil infiltration — no neutrophil-restricted granule protein or marker (ELANE, MPO, PRTN3, defensins, LTF, CAMP, LCN2, S100A8/A9, FCGR3B, CSF3R) is present among the 118 genes, the 22 driver genes are all microglia/macrophage transcripts including TYROBP, CD68, CTSD, CTSS, GRN and CHIT1, and neutrophils rank only 34th among cell-type terms. The Reactome set was annotated largely from neutrophil granule proteomics and is best read as shared myeloid lysosomal/secretory machinery. This is mechanistically apt: several members are secreted lysosomal products with established relevance to ALS and frontotemporal dementia, including progranulin and chitotriosidase, the latter a CSF biomarker of microglial activation. The oligodendrocyte cluster is confirmed functionally by GO myelination (4 genes, FDR = 4 × 10⁻⁵) together with cell–cell and adherens junction terms consistent with axo-glial junction components such as NFASC. The astrocyte cluster, by contrast, shows essentially no pathway-level enrichment — across GO, KEGG, Reactome, Hallmark and WikiPathways its only hit is arginine and proline metabolism (3 genes, FDR = 2 × 10⁻³) — so it is defined by cell-type identity rather than by a coherent biological process, consistent with it being the least internally coherent of the three clusters.

**Interpretation.** The genes nominated by the mediation model are not 118 independent mediators but approximately three coordinated cell-type programs, of which the myeloid program is by far the largest, the most internally coherent and the most functionally specific. Gene-level mediation testing across these 118 genes therefore re-tests a small number of latent variables many times over; the three cluster eigengenes are the appropriate unit for mediation analysis. A caveat applies to the myeloid cluster at this resolution: it contains nine ribosomal protein genes, detectable here as a KEGG ribosome enrichment (7 genes, FDR = 5 × 10⁻⁵), which form a distinct sub-cluster when the dendrogram is cut at *k* = 4. Whether that sub-cluster reflects a translation-related program — a mechanism of interest given that *C9orf72* dipeptide repeats impair ribosome function — or variation in RNA integrity cannot be resolved from these data and requires adjustment for RIN and post-mortem interval.
